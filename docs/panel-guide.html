1
Upload test — the number that matters

Every 20 s the tool pushes an 8 MB file through the Mudi to Google Cloud Storage and times it. This is the real upload speed at the spot you are in right now.

Colour: green ≥ 80, yellow ≥ 40, red below 40. Your 33 is red because the upload carrier is weak (see 4).

2
Last tests

One bar per test, newest on the right, same colours as above. Shows the trend as you drive: rising bars mean you are heading the right way.

3
Network

What the modem is connected to. 5G NSA = LTE anchor with a 5G add-on carrier; 5G SA = pure 5G, the best case for upload; LTE = 4G only.

4
Upload carrier — the line to watch

The primary carrier. Your upload data travels on this one carrier, whatever else is aggregated. Wider is faster: 20 MHz green, 15 yellow, 10 or less red.

Here it is B8 at 10 MHz, a low band with half the width of B3 at 20 MHz. That alone caps upload well below what the same spot gives on B3. A good spot is one where this line reads B3 · 20 MHz or n78.

5
All carriers

Every carrier the modem has bonded, primary first. Mostly a download story; kept small so you can see when the set changes.

6
Signal strength, RSRP

How loud the tower is, in dBm. Closer to zero is better: ≥ -80 green, ≥ -95 yellow, weaker red. -59 is excellent. PCI is the cell's ID; when it changes you have been handed to another cell.

7
Signal quality, SINR

Signal versus noise and neighbouring cells, in dB. This predicts upload better than RSRP: ≥ 15 green, ≥ 5 yellow, below red. Strong RSRP with poor SINR means interference, usually between two towers.

8
5G carrier

The 5G add-on: band, width and its own SINR. n1 at 20 MHz adds little for upload. A spot showing n78 with 80 to 100 MHz would be the prize.

9
Position and sample count

Where the position comes from (the modem's GPS, or the browser while indoors) and how many 2-second samples have been logged to the CSV.

10
Controls

follow keeps the map on you · probe now runs an extra test immediately · color switches the track between upload speed and SINR · center + zoom jumps to street level · browser GPS uses the laptop or phone location instead of the modem.

What a good spot looks like

Upload carrier B3 · 20 MHz, or n78

The single biggest factor. Drive until line 4 turns green, then look for the best test result within that area.

SINR above 15 dB

Strong signal is not enough; a spot between two towers can have -60 dBm and still upload slowly. Line 7 tells you.

Stable cell, stable numbers

Park where the PCI stops changing and three tests in a row agree. The best-spots table on the right ranks 60 m cells by their best test.
